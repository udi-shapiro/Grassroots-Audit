# Calculus (18) against GSC (19) and Compiling GC (3)

27 November 2026 · Calculus project · Grassroots Review

**Verdict.** The removal of `met` still supports interaction between separate components containing persons. The new truth rule also fixes negated ground tests. The new predicate and quantifier rules need two repairs before Definition 8 gives the claimed operational semantics for every contract. One affects Lemma 7 directly. The other affects which transactions a clause can induce. The catalogue and the compiler paper also allow role identifications that the revised calculus excludes.

## 1. Computable predicates can invalidate Lemma 7

**Finding 32 · §§3.1–3.2, Lemma 7 and Appendix A, pp. 5–6 and 13.** Let `ok` be a computable predicate true in every local state. Let `knows` and `mark` be primitive. Consider these two unguarded clauses.

```text
¬x:ok, y:mark ← x:knows(y), x:ok, ¬y:mark.
       y:mark ←                    ¬y:mark.
```

Take distinct persons Alice and Bob for $x$ and $y$, respectively. Alice records Bob, has no stored `ok` atom, and Bob has no `mark`. Both bodies hold. Both agents of the binary clause have a literal in the syntactic change set $D(C)$. Its head deletes nothing at Alice and adds `mark` at Bob. The unary transaction at Bob has the very same transition in its closure over Alice and Bob. The transactions have different participant sets, so they are distinct. This contradicts Lemma 7. Definition 8 invokes that lemma to discharge VMAT's condition on unguarded transaction classes. The binary clause even has a connected recording graph. Adding Knowledge's Establish and Acquaint makes the example state reachable and supplies Introduction.

**Repair.** Make every head literal primitive and the explicit local store a finite set of ground primitive atoms. Keep computable literals in bodies, at either polarity. Require every such literal to satisfy the intended variable condition at clause formation. Page 6 defines “proper” for a computable atom and never requires it. Give `person` and symmetric/reflexive declarations their intended primitive status. The proof can then infer an actual set change from each witness in $D(C)$, with the existing restriction on updates to reflexive self-atoms. Compiling GC (3), Lemma 1.5, needs the same edit. [Finding 32](https://github.com/udi-shapiro/Grassroots-Audit/issues/211)

**Earlier Finding 31 is resolved.** A ground computable literal and its negation now test complementary truth values in the same local model (§3.2). In particular, an always-true unstored test passes positively and fails negatively. The binary example above exercises a different operation, the deletion of an atom from a store. [Earlier Finding 31](https://github.com/udi-shapiro/Grassroots-Audit/issues/201)

## 2. A variable local to negation needs its own evaluation rule

**Finding 33 · Definitions 3, 5, 6 and 12(2), §§6.3 and 7.** Take a primitive `tag` with argument type $\{0,1\}$, and the clause `x:mark ← ¬x:tag(z)`, guarded by $x$. Suppose `tag(0)` is the only tag at $x$, and `mark` is absent. Definition 3 gives the condition “there exists no $z$ with `tag(z)`”. That is false. If Definitions 5–6 instantiate every variable and evaluate a ground literal, the instance $z=1$ passes and adds `mark`. The choice of interpretation therefore changes the transition relation. Definition 6 gives a truth rule for the grounded literal but no operational rule for the local binder. The holder $x$ is fixed in this example, and the domain has only two values.

**Repair.** Ground the free variables and determine the participants and guards from that substitution. For each negative body pattern, test absence of any matching tuple for its locally bound variables in the designated holder's model. State the scope of repeated variables within a pattern and standardize local variables of separate negatives apart. Require holders and guards to be fixed before this test, and forbid local variables in effects. Define the permitted fragment of computable tests so that evaluation is decidable. Total evaluation on one ground tuple alone does not decide an unbounded existential search. An alternative already used in Compiling GC (3), §1, is to require variables of negative or computable body literals to be bound by the head or a positive primitive literal as specified there, and express an absence query as a declared computable test of the fixed holder and its free parameters. In that case specify the translation of the Calculus construct.

**There is a consequence for augmentation.** Add just the positive condition `x:eligible(z)`, with only `eligible(1)` present. If variable scope is recomputed from the combined clause, $z$ now occurs positively. The augmented instance $z=1$ can fire even though the original clause cannot. To justify §7's statement that adding a condition restricts when an act may be taken, make augmentation capture-avoiding. Alpha-rename the original local binder before adding a condition that uses its spelling. The two-valued example tests both readings. [Finding 33](https://github.com/udi-shapiro/Grassroots-Audit/issues/212)

## 3. Ground roles and the compiler's input language

**Definitions 5–6 are now injective on all distinct agent variables.** This gives the restricted core a determinate default. It removes the former exception for declared coincidences. The published Notes:3 Pay specifically allows the issuer Carol to be the payer Alice or the recipient Bob. With only Alice and Bob present, Alice can pay Bob an Alice-note under the catalogue declaration. Its three agent variables admit no proper instance under Definition 5. The names, expanded implication and its two permitted equalities are explicit in the source. GSC (19), Definition 5, and Compiling GC (3), Definition 1.3, also provide for declared coincidences, subject to their respective conditions. [Notes names and Pay schema](https://github.com/EShapiro2/GLP/blob/7b68b74c6388135736632957b321ae63503feaf7/calculus/contracts/Notes/full-schemas.txt#L14)

The injective core can be retained. Elaborate each source clause into one clause per permitted equality partition, identify variables and guards, combine the effects and apply the usual conflict and well-formedness checks. State the source and target instance correspondence, including the transaction classes used for volition. Keep source identities and declarations available to the inverse map where exact recovery is claimed. Alternatively restore the permission as clause data in both maps and in inheritance. Finding 21 recurs at the grounding rule. Finding 30 remains open for translating the catalogue. [Grounding finding](https://github.com/udi-shapiro/Grassroots-Audit/issues/98) · [Translation finding](https://github.com/udi-shapiro/Grassroots-Audit/issues/200)

The compiler paper also universally quantifies the variables in its implications and imposes a range condition on **negative as well as computable** body literals. Its operational semantics cannot be imported verbatim for the negative-only variable above. Figure 2 still cites the older OSPM for the arrows through GLP. The replacement paper's Theorem 7.1 reaches vGLP for effective contracts under its stated conditions. Identify the common source fragment or its translation, and cite the bridge for the revised vGLP interface before claiming that the replacement proves the entire path to GLP. ## 4. The scope of Theorem 21 and the comparison with GSC

The revised abstract correctly excludes groups that have already interacted through others. If each of two distinct interaction components contains an established person, Lemma 24 implies that neither person records the other. An introductory act then adds a genuinely new record at one person and consumes the other's willingness. Appendix A's extension argument gives the claimed interaction without a dedicated `met` predicate. This argument applies once the operational semantics is well-defined. The change from “syntactically grassroots” to “grassroots conditions” also accommodates the semantic preservation obligation in Definition 19(2).

Definition 18(2) actually also covers some persons in one component when a record is missing and there has been no prior interaction across the specified groups. GSC (19), Definition 25(1), asks for first interaction from every finite safe prefix without a prior direct interaction of the pair. In the public Knowledge example, Carol separately acquaints herself with Alice and Bob and introduces each to the other. Alice and Bob have never interacted directly, and all distinct pairs now record one another. I replayed the trace. All 15 final ground instances exclude a further transaction. The change of title in Definition 18 and Theorem 21 to “Grassroots” leaves this distinction in place. When comparing results across papers, qualify the use of the approved short name, for example “grassroots in the sense of Definition 18 (for persons)”. [Verified trace](./knowledge_three_people.py) · [GSC scope finding](https://github.com/udi-shapiro/Grassroots-Audit/issues/169)

**Full GSC subsumption remains unproved.** Section 6.3 explicitly leaves role-valued guards and universal clearing outside the logic. Set-valued stores still need a translation or a stated restriction for multiset production and witnessing (GSC Proposition 42). The general Polite and active-ward results, signed undertakings and presenting runs, counter-machine simulation, and time/place initialization and updates still need an identified home or a corresponding result. A short scope table could identify which remain in GSC. [Itemized comparison](./Calculus_15_GSC_19_Review.md) · [Project's scope response](https://github.com/udi-shapiro/Grassroots-Audit/issues/168#issuecomment-7203619958)
