# Compiling Grassroots Contracts into vGLP — review of revision (4)

28 November 2026 · Compiling GC (4), 27 pages · Calculus (19), 15 pages · OSPM full version, 82 pages

**Verdict.** The project answered the revision (3) review. Definition 4.4 excludes the original undecidable-membership example. Calculus (19) also repairs the primitive-head defect, but Compiling GC (4) still prints the old rule. More importantly, the two current manuscripts assign different transitions and different liveness classes to the same small contracts. Theorem 7.1 therefore does not yet give a compilation theorem for the current Calculus semantics. I also found a residual decidability problem in Stand and a false statement about overlapping standing requests in the new Cancel proof. Each has a short repair.

## 1. Match both the transitions and the classes of Calculus (19)

**Different transitions, even with primitive atoms, one agent, and no negation.** Take two unary implications guarded by p. A setup implication adds `person` and `a` to the empty state. The second has body `p:person` and head `p:a, p:b`. Once setup has run, p holds exactly {person, a}. Give p the will required for the second implication.

| At {person, a} | Result of the second implication |
|---|---|
| Calculus (19), Definition 7, p. 6 | Disabled. Adding a would leave that literal unchanged. Every head literal must change the state. |
| Compiling GC (4), Definition 2.4, p. 2 | Enabled. The resulting state {person, a, b} differs from the old state. |
| Compiling GC 4.1–4.2, 4.9(1) | The unary act passes the local-domain and changed-state checks and can decide. |

The discrepancy is reachable and survives the effectiveness conditions in 4.4. It concerns what a compiled person can do with consent. **Repair:** import Calculus Definition 7 and put the literal-by-literal tests into each local domain in 4.1. Require every positive head atom to be absent and every negative head atom to be present at its holder. Recheck Lemma 4.2, the changed vote, and the clauses implementing the protocol’s own record updates. An idempotent record update needs to emit only its actual additions and deletions under this rule.

**Different classes, even where all enabled steps agree.** Consider just two unary implications, both guarded by p and both adding `p:done`. One has body `p:flag`, the other `¬p:flag`. Consider their transactions at states where `done` is absent. Calculus Definition 9 gives them **one** class because the participants and ground head coincide. Compiling GC 2.6 equates transactions generated by the same ground instance and closes that relation under transitivity. Here the two instances have disjoint transaction sets because their bodies are mutually exclusive. They give **two** classes. A person’s request, its fulfilment and the scheduler’s fairness obligation can therefore differ even when the visible machine update is the same.

**Repair:** import Definition 9 as well as Definition 7. Carry the revised source class through 4.3, Request, Stand, Cancel and 4.19. In the platform mapping 6.3, identify precisely which protocol approval class each request induces. Recheck the grouping of protocol transitions in 4.13 and the claims about fulfilment in A.9 and A.14 against that equivalence. If the paper deliberately compiles a restricted fragment, state the restriction and prove agreement of the two semantics on it.

**The primitive-head witness still applies to Compiling GC.** Let `ok` be computable and true everywhere. Use the unguarded implications `¬p:ok, q:mark ← p:peer(q), p:ok, ¬q:mark` and `q:mark ← ¬q:mark`. When p stores just `peer(q)` and q is empty, deleting the unstored `ok` does nothing. Both implications induce the same transition over {p,q} after closure, although their participants differ. Calculus (19) now excludes the binary implication. Adopting its primitive-head rule resolves this case in Compiling GC too. It leaves the two preceding, entirely primitive examples to be addressed.

## 2. Stand still needs an emptiness test when the initiator is locked

Definition 4.7 makes p free for a class exactly when p holds no lock and has no pending question of that class. Stand in 4.9(3) requires freedom for **every** class in the named request. At a locked p, this condition holds exactly when the request denotes the empty set. Definition 4.4(7) decides membership. Condition (8) enumerates the acts whose domains contain the current local state. Neither decides whether the entire denotation is empty.

Here is an effective contract witnessing the distinction. For each natural n let Uₙ be the class of the guarded unary act `p:mark(n) ← p:tag(n), ¬p:mark(n)`, guarded by p. One implication supplies all these instances. Each class is a singleton at the level of acts, since different n add different atoms. Include a separate guarded binary act so that p can reach a state with an undecided lock. Give p the valid, identifier-free standing-request names `r(e)`. Put Uₙ in `r(e)` precisely when machine e halts for the first time at step n. Membership is decidable by n steps of simulation. For condition (8), inspect the finitely many tags stored at p, test membership for each n, and discard marked ones. There need be no unguarded source implication. The other effectiveness requirements can be met with ordinary decidable data and trivial constraints.

At the same reachable locked state, with no prior `r(e)` pending, the local precondition of `Stand(r(e))` is true precisely when machine e **never** halts. All names are admitted, and the variable part e is an integer. Thus the remaining problem persists with decidable membership, finite local enumeration, and even denotations of cardinality at most one.

**Smallest repair:** require an empty lock explicitly for Stand. Once the lock is empty, check whether any of the finitely many pending question classes belongs to the request. This admits exactly the same nonempty requests as the current rule and changes only when a vacuous request can be installed. Alternative repairs are to require every admitted request to be nonempty or to supply a uniform emptiness test.

One further convention is needed for the claim that *all* preconditions are decidable. Section 4 fixes a total order on identifiers. Definition 4.8 evaluates that order when two initiators have the same rank, including when two starts wait at the same participant. Specify a computable total order on identifier encodings. Countability and totality alone do not supply the comparison procedure.

## 3. Cancel and overlapping standing requests

Definition 4.12(16) allows the initiator to cancel its guarded attempt while it holds the lock and the attempt is undecided. It closes the attempt as declined. The competing last-yes Tally can decide only an open attempt. If Cancel happens first, Tally ignores the closed attempt. If decision happens first, Cancel’s precondition fails. Remote participants receive the initiator’s abort. The distinction between declined and unable is carried through 4.9(1) and A.8, so a cancellation does not impose the readiness requirement for a failed machine precondition.

**Lemma A.6, p. 15, is false for requests covering multiple classes.** Let p stand on a request ρ covering U and V. Let U be a guarded two-person act initiated by p so its attempt can be locked and undecided. Before any commit, p cancels that attempt. Rule (16) ends the *whole* standing request ρ. Its standing approval for V therefore ends, even though nobody withdrew ρ, cancelled V, or committed V. A.6 lists just those three events for V.

**Repair:** say that a standing approval for V supplied by ρ can end when p cancels an attempt of **any class in ρ**, when ρ is withdrawn, or when a committed V attempt is closed at p. A.7 still gets the required persistence: while the guard holds lock α it can Cancel only α, and only as its initiator. A.9(5) should note that such a closing can change several classes of the one agent’s projected volition in one step. The machine state is unchanged. The completeness construction’s choice to take no Cancel remains available. A fresh ordinary request can express a later change of mind.

This semantics also determines what a person cancels. If ρ covers both buying and selling, cancelling an outstanding buy can end the standing authorization to sell. If that is intended, the correction is in A.6 and its explanation. If cancellation should affect only the buy class, rule (16) needs a class-specific record instead.
